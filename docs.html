Setting up Combo Guard

Everything you can configure, from the first minute to a fully tuned group. Most options live in one menu; the rest are short commands listed below.

Getting started

  1. Add the bot. Open @Combo_Guard_bot and pick your group, or add it from your group's member list.
  2. Make it an admin. The bot needs permission to ban users, delete messages, and create invite links. Without admin rights it can see problems but not act on them.
  3. Open the menu. Send /menu in the group, or DM the bot and pick the group from My Connected Groups.
Protection defaults are on immediately. Scam phrases, phishing domains, impersonation checks, restricted name tags, and image scanning all run from the moment the bot becomes admin. Everything below is tuning, not required setup.

The control panel

Everything below is configured from /menu in a DM with the bot — pick your group, then a section. Every command has a menu equivalent, so nothing is hidden behind memorized syntax.

Send /menu in a group (or in DM) to open the per-group control panel. From there you can toggle and configure:

  • Anti-spam toggles for each protection type: scam phrases, contract addresses, external links and mentions, forwarded messages, invite-link phrases.
  • Custom restricted words on top of the built-in list.
  • Allowlists: your own token's contract address, trusted forward sources, and invite phrases your group actually uses.
  • Restricted name tags: extend the default list (admin, support, dev, and similar) with your own.
  • Escalation ladder: how warnings turn into mutes, kicks, and bans.
  • New-member media restriction: stop fresh joins from posting photos and videos until they have earned it.
  • Welcome message, portal, captcha, logging, and daily summary settings, covered in their own sections below.

Protections

These run on every message: text, captions, the text inside images, and video keyframes. Display names are re-checked on every message too, so renaming mid-session does not work.

What gets caught

  • Scam phrases: a curated library of crypto-scam lines ("holders dm me", seed-phrase bait, signal sellers, CA-redeploy/airdrop-registration scripts) plus pattern templates. Unicode disguises and spaced-out letters are normalized before matching. A combo layer also flags messages that pair a distribution claim ("airdropping holders", "redeploying the CA") with a contact-me solicitation ("DM me", "send me a PM") — the skeleton of the airdrop-registration scam, caught even when every individual phrase is reworded.
  • Phishing domains: a built-in blocklist enforced in every group, extendable per group with your own restricted words.
  • Contract addresses: ETH and Solana addresses in messages, names, or images. Whitelist your own project's address in /menu; everything else escalates.
  • Wallet screenshots: images are read with OCR and scored against a wallet-UI model. Known spam images and videos are fingerprinted, so a repost means an instant ban.
  • Impersonators: names compared against your real admin list and the group title, with lookalike characters normalized and a fuzzy-match tolerance. Banned on sight — at join and on rename. When a rename is involved, the group gets a public callout showing the before→after names (see Name Protection).
  • Restricted name tags: role words like admin, support, dev in display names and usernames. Joining with one is allowed but flagged to your log channel; renaming to one after joining is an instant ban — see Name Protection for the full policy and per-tag controls.

Warnings and escalation

Lighter offenses earn a warning first. Every warning comes with a 10-second Undo button for the acting admin, and /remove_warn (reply to the user, or pass a user id) clears all active warnings at once. Repeat offenses climb the ladder you configured: warn, mute, kick, ban.

The global banlist

High-confidence detections (spam media, impersonation, restricted name tags) are banned in every group the bot guards, not just where they were caught. Only the bot's own detections feed this list automatically. /gban lets a group admin push a confirmed bad actor to it; /ban stays local to your group.

Purge on ban

When an admin manually bans a member — natively or via /ban — the bot automatically deletes that member's recent messages from the group (everything it tracked over the last 48 hours). Toggle per group in /menu → 🧹 Purge on Ban; on by default.

Every manual ban also offers the admins a one-tap "add to global banlist" button — on the ban confirmation and in the log channel. The write only happens on that explicit, attributed tap, so a single hasty ban never propagates network-wide by itself.

Locking the chat

/lock freezes the group in one tap (with a confirm button): non-admins can't send anything until /unlock. The bot snapshots the group's exact permissions before locking and restores them on unlock — a custom setup (say, media disabled) comes back precisely as it was.

Whitelist

Trusted members skip the checks. /whitelist (reply to the user, or pass their id) exempts them from content moderation and the flood guard, and clears their active warnings; /unwhitelist reverses it and /whitelisted lists everyone. Members can also earn a lighter automatic whitelist: 10 meaningful messages spread across at least 2 different days — filler like "gm" counts for nothing. Auto-whitelisted members keep going through the high-confidence image and video scanners; nobody, whitelisted or not, ever skips the global banlist or impersonation detection, and any punishment revokes the whitelist instantly.

Name Protection

Open /menu → 🪪 Name Protection. This page controls how the bot treats display names and usernames that carry protected role words or match your admins' names.

The policy: joining with a role word ≠ renaming to one

Plenty of legitimate members have role words in their names — "Alex | Solidity Dev", "CEO @ SomeStartup". Banning them at the door costs you real members. But someone who joins as "Innocent Bob" and later renames to "Dev Support" is unambiguously hostile. The bot keeps a per-member name history (recorded at join, refreshed on each message), which lets it tell these two cases apart:

  • Joins with a tag in their name → allowed in, with a quiet flag to your log channel: "Name-tag flag: joined carrying 'dev' — allowed, watching for rename-to-tag." No public callout, no ban.
  • Renames to a tag after joining → immediate ban, global-banlist propagation, and a public group callout with the before→after names:
    ⚠️ Impersonation blocked — [user] was previously named "Alex" and renamed to "Dev Mark" (restricted role tag "dev"). They have been banned. If this account messaged you privately, do not trust it — admins never DM first.
    The callout is the point: members who were DM'd by the fake "Dev" can recognize the name and know it was a scam.
  • Existing members who always carried a tag (they predate the bot) → flagged once to the log channel, then left alone. No repeat alerts, no bans.
  • Admin-name or group-name matches → always banned on sight, at join and on rename. Nobody arrives with your admin's exact display name by accident. Lookalike Unicode characters and digit substitutions are folded before comparing.

Controls on the page

  • 🏷️ Restricted role tags and 🕵️ Admin/group impersonation — independent on/off switches for the two detection families.
  • 🚪 Join action — the join-time policy for tag hits: 🔔 Alert (allow) is the default; switch to ⛔ Ban to restore the hard line for high-security groups.
  • ⚙️ Built-in tags — all 16 built-in role words (owner, founder, ceo, cto, admin, moderator, mod, support, team, official, dev, developer, creator, manager, staff, helper) as individual per-group toggles. Running a developer community? Switch off dev there while keeping support and official armed.
  • ➕ Add custom tag — extend the list with your own words (e.g. treasury, listing). One word, letters/digits, matched the same way as built-ins. Each custom tag gets a one-tap remove button.

Tags match whole words only — a member named "Modjuh" is fine ("mod" is embedded mid-word), "Dev Mark" hits. Unicode lookalikes (Cyrillic letters, fullwidth forms, math alphabets) are normalized before matching.

Where the join flags go

Alerts land in your log channel under the automated category. Tip: /setlog accepts a private admin group id, not just a channel — point it at your mod chat and the team can discuss each flag right where it appears. If no log channel is set, the Name Protection page shows a reminder.

One platform limitation to know

Telegram does not notify bots when a user renames themselves. A rename is only visible on the user's next activity in the group — so a scammer who renames and then only sends private DMs is invisible to any bot. The public callout exists precisely for the moment they do surface in the group.

Anti-Spam menu — row by row

Open /menu → 🚫 Anti-Spam. Every protection is its own row showing 🟢/🔴 (on/off) and mode/limit (e.g. warn/3 means "three warnings, then apply the mode's escalation"). Tap a row to change its mode/limit or flip it on/off.

  • Contract address in username — catches new members whose display name or username contains a token contract address. Common promotion tactic: rename to 0xabc… to advertise a token in every group they type in. Default: ban/1 (single strike, immediate ban) because there is no legitimate reason to have a CA in your name.
  • Contract address in chat — catches any message that pastes a contract address as text, in a caption, or embedded in an image (via OCR). Your own project's address is exempt via the CA Allowlist below. Default: warn/3.
  • Quoted links — catches messages where the link is smuggled inside a Telegram quote / reply-preview block, a classic way to slip past regex-based link blockers. Default: warn/3.
  • External replies — catches replies whose "reply-to" points at a message in a different chat (i.e. the user is dragging a message from another group into yours as bait). Default: warn/3.
  • External mentions — catches @username mentions of accounts that are not members of the current group. Prevents drive-by tagging campaigns and out-of-band solicitation. Default: warn/3.
  • Forwarded messages — catches messages forwarded in from another chat / channel. Common vector for signal-service and pump-scheme spam. Trusted sources go on the Forward Source Allowlist (below). Default: warn/3.
  • Blacklisted words in message — catches messages containing any phrase from the built-in scam-phrase library (holders-dm-me lines, seed-phrase bait, signal sellers, phishing domains, invite-link phrases). Extend the list per group under 📝 Manage restricted words. Default: warn/3.

Mode and limit

Every row's mode is one of:

  • warn — issue a warning; on hitting the limit, apply the escalation mode set for that protection (temp-mute → mute → kick → ban, depending on your ladder).
  • temp_mute — mute the user for a fixed duration on the first offense past the limit.
  • mute — silence permanently once the limit is reached.
  • kick — remove from the group; user can rejoin.
  • ban — remove from the group; blocked from rejoining. ban is the only mode that also propagates the user to the cross-group banlist (bot-detected only; admin-manual bans stay local).

The /limit number is how many strikes trigger the mode. ban/1 means one strike is enough; warn/3 means three warnings, then escalate. Warnings are cleared with the 🔄 Undo warning button on each warning post (10-second window) or /remove_warn for bulk recovery.

CA Allowlist (project's CA)

Exempts specific contract addresses from the "contract address in chat" protection above. Add your project's own token contract here — otherwise members quoting or pasting it get warned. Marker: ➕ when empty, ✅ (N) once you have added entries. The row also shows the count so you can see at a glance how many are on the list.

Forward Source Allowlist

Exempts specific source channels from the "forwarded messages" protection above. Use it for partner-project announcements, official news feeds, and similar channels whose forwards you want to permit. The group's natively-linked discussion channel is auto-allowed in code, so you do not need an entry for that. Same marker convention (➕ empty, ✅ (N) populated).

Allow Telegram invite links

The built-in phrase blocker treats t.me/+…, t.me/joinchat/…, and "join @channel" as a blacklisted phrase — invite links are a top spam vector. If your group is a cross-community space where members legitimately share invite links, flip this to 🟢 ON to remove that phrase from the block set. Default: 🔴 OFF.

Manage restricted words

Shortcut to the per-group restricted-words editor. Same page as /menu → 🛡️ Image Guard → 📝 Restricted Words, just discoverable from where admins look for blacklisted-text policy. Anything you add here augments the built-in scam-phrase library and is enforced by "blacklisted words in message" above.

Prices and charts

Members can pull a live price card or a candlestick chart for any token straight from the chat. Listed coins resolve through CoinGecko and Binance; DEX-traded tokens come from DexScreener across eleven chains (Ethereum, BSC, Polygon, Arbitrum, Optimism, Avalanche, Base, Solana, Fantom, Cronos, Linea).

Commands

  • /p <symbol or CA> [chain] gives a price card with market cap, 24-hour move, and volume. Example: /p btc.
  • /c <symbol or CA> [chain] [1h|4h|1d|1w] renders a candlestick chart. The timeframe defaults to 4h. Example: /c eth 1d.
  • Paste a contract address instead of a symbol to resolve a token directly: /p 0xd8E1….

Project token (bare /p and /c)

Pin your group's own token so members get its price and chart without typing a symbol or address:

  1. Open /menu and choose 💹 Price Commands, then 🪙 Project token, then Set project token.
  2. Paste the contract address (EVM 0x… or Solana base58).
  3. The bot looks it up, picks the highest-liquidity chain, shows a preview, and asks you to confirm.

After that, a bare /p or /c in the group shows the project token instantly. One chain per group; if your token trades on several, pick the main pool. It also works in DM once you have opened /menu for that group.

Safety and control

  • Clone-aware chooser: when a symbol is ambiguous, the bot lists candidates with chain badges and flags suspicious clones (very new, tiny liquidity) with a warning, so members do not land on a copycat.
  • Canonical first: /p btc shows Bitcoin, not a DEX wrapper. Listed coins win the default slot, with a "Different one?" button for wrapped variants.
  • Per-group toggle: the whole feature is on by default. Disable it from /menu → 💹 Price Commands in groups you want kept strictly moderation-only.

Portal and captcha

The portal screens people before they enter, instead of cleaning up after them.

  1. In your group's Telegram settings, enable Approve New Members.
  2. Create a channel to act as the portal, add the bot to it, and send /id there to get the channel id.
  3. Back in the group, send /setportal_channel <channel_id>. Use /unsetportal_channel to turn it off.

Every join request is then checked against the global banlist, restricted name tags, contract addresses in the name, and impersonation of your admins or group name. Clean requests are approved; flagged ones are declined with a short audit note in the portal channel.

Captcha options

  • Math challenge: a small arithmetic prompt the joiner answers in the portal channel.
  • Image captcha: the joiner types the text shown in an image.
  • Off: instant approval once the screening passes.

The captcha type and the timeout (default 5 minutes, then the request expires) are set per group in /menu.

Landing message and Join button

Once a portal channel is connected the bot posts a landing message there with a 🚪 Join <group> URL button on top plus any extra buttons you've configured. The Join button routes tap-throughs via the bot's screening and captcha, so it can't be bypassed by pasting the raw invite link.

  • Everything is edited from /menu → 🚪 Portal → the Greeting sub-menu: greeting text (with the same placeholders as welcome messages), an optional photo or video, and your own extra buttons.
  • The Join button's label is auto-generated from the group's title. If you renamed the group and the label is stuck on the old name, or you just want different capitalization, open 🔘 Buttons and tap 🚪 Edit Join button. Type any label (up to 64 characters, emoji allowed) and the landing message is re-posted automatically with the new button — no need to delete and rebuild the portal.
  • Media in the greeting is cached after the first send, so re-posting after every text edit does not re-upload the whole photo or video from the server.

Raid Shield

Two independent detectors, both off by default, configured from /menu → 🛡️ Raid Shield.

Flood guard

Someone posting a burst (default 8 messages in 10 seconds, presets from strict to relaxed) has the burst deleted and goes through the group's normal warning ladder — the same warn → mute → ban escalation as any other violation, with the same undo button and silent-mode behavior.

Raid guard

A join cascade (default 10 joins in 60 seconds) triggers raid mode — immediately in Auto mode, or after an admin taps the button on the alert. Activation is retroactive on the wave that tripped it. While active, every new joiner is muted and pointed at a one-tap human check: press Start in the bot's DM and you're chatting seconds later. Bots can't press it. Joiners who already passed the portal captcha are exempt. Raid mode expires on its own, or an admin taps All clear and chooses whether the never-verified leftovers get unmuted or removed.

Launching something and expecting a genuine wave of joins? The 📈 Expecting a wave button pauses both detectors for 2–24 hours so real growth is never treated as an attack.

Welcome messages

Greet new members with text, a photo, or a video, configured in /menu. Welcome text supports placeholders:

  • {first_name}, {full_name}, {username}, {group_name}

You can attach your own buttons to the welcome message: links, or menu buttons that open in place, with control over which buttons share a row. Media is cached after the first send, so big welcome videos do not slow the bot down.

Hashtag menus

Reusable button menus any member can call up by typing #name in chat. Building one is admin-only:

  • /savemenu <name> as a reply to a message: that message becomes the menu intro.
  • /addbutton <menu> <label>|<content> adds a button. Append |same to keep it on the same row as the previous button.
  • /menus lists menus, /delbutton and /delmenu remove things.

Typical uses: #rules, #links, #faq, #buy.

Filters

Custom auto-replies. When a message matches a trigger, the bot answers with your configured response: text, image, video, or sticker, with optional buttons.

  • /filter <trigger> <response> creates one. Triggers can be plain words or patterns.
  • /filters lists them, /stop <trigger> deletes one, /edit_filter <trigger> <new> updates one.
  • /testfilter <trigger> in DM previews the response without posting it in the group. See Testing filters in DM below.

Testing filters in DM

Plain triggers only fire inside the group they belong to — if you type the trigger in your DM with the bot, nothing happens. That is intentional: it stops casual DM messages from accidentally posting filter responses.

To preview a filter privately, use the dedicated command:

/testfilter <trigger>

It resolves the group you last opened via /menu, looks up the trigger against that group's filters, and delivers the response right there in the DM. The reply uses the same render path as in-group replies — text vs image vs video, inline buttons, formatting — so what you see in DM is exactly what members would see in the group.

The leading slash on the trigger is optional. Both of these work:

/testfilter rules
/testfilter /rules

If you manage several groups, /testfilter targets whichever group you last opened via /menu. Switch between them from /start → My Connected Groups → 🎛️ Open Panel on the group you want to test against.

Media filters

Reply to a photo or video with /filter <trigger> /image or /filter <trigger> /video to attach that media as the response. Add an optional caption after the marker: /filter logo /image Our official mark, please share the source.

Inline buttons

Any text response (or media caption) can carry tappable URL buttons instead of raw links. Use this Markdown-style marker inside the response:

[Button Label](buttonurl:https://your-site.com)

The bot strips the marker out of the visible text and renders Button Label as an inline button underneath the reply. Nothing shows up as a bare URL in the message body.

Full example

/filter rules Please read the rules before posting.

[📚 Open Rules](buttonurl:https://comboguard.vercel.app/rules)

Typing rules in the group produces "Please read the rules before posting." plus a tappable 📚 Open Rules button that links to your site.

Multiple buttons

Add more markers — each starts a new row by default. Append :same to a URL to keep the button on the same row as the previous one; rows wrap automatically after three buttons.

/filter socials Follow us:

[🌐 Website](buttonurl:https://comboguard.vercel.app)
[🐦 X](buttonurl:https://x.com/comboguard:same)
[✈️ Telegram](buttonurl:https://t.me/comboguard:same)

Result: one row with three buttons — Website · X · Telegram.

Supported URL schemes

  • https:// and http:// — standard web links.
  • tg:// — Telegram deep links such as tg://user?id=123456789.
  • Bare domains are auto-prefixed with https://, so [Docs](buttonurl:comboguard.vercel.app/docs.html) works too.

Editing an existing filter works the same way — just include the button markers in the new response passed to /edit_filter.

Timed messages

Recurring announcements posted on an interval, useful for rules reminders or pinned-info refreshers. Everything is managed from /menu → ⏰ Timed Messages: a guided add flow (pick the interval, type Title | text), and a per-message page with a preview, pause/resume, edit, interval change, and delete.

  • Command alternative: /timed_add <minutes> <title>|<text> schedules one.
  • /timed_list shows them with their index numbers; /timed_remove <idx> deletes, /timed_toggle <idx> pauses and resumes.

Logging channel

Route a full audit trail into a private channel only your admin team can see.

  1. Create a private channel and add the bot.
  2. Send /id in the channel to get its id.
  3. In the group, send /setlog <channel_id>. Check with /logchannel, disconnect with /unsetlog.

Tip — use a group instead of a channel: /setlog accepts any chat the bot can post to, including a private group where your mod team hangs out. Alerts then arrive as normal chat messages the team can reply to and discuss in place — handy for the Name Protection join flags, which are informational rather than pure audit records.

Each category can be switched on and off with /log <category> and /nolog <category>:

Daily summary

Every evening at 20:00, active groups get a short digest of the last 24 hours: bans, mutes, warnings, cleanup, and new arrivals compared with the day before. Groups with nothing to report are skipped, so quiet days stay quiet. The summary can be disabled per group in /menu.

Referral campaigns

A built-in growth system: members share personal invite links, and the bot verifies the people they bring actually participate.

Running a campaign (admins)

  • /setupcampaign starts one and walks you through duration and rewards.
  • /setbanner and /setendbanner attach start and end images, set up in DM.
  • /setresultschat picks the channel where standings are posted; /lastresult reposts the latest.
  • /endcampaign closes it manually. /adminhelp lists all campaign admin commands.
  • Borderline cases can be reviewed with /refdetails, /refstats, and decided with /refvalidate or /refinvalidate.
  • /refaudit (also in /menu → Referral System → Admin Tools) generates an anti-bot report: every referral gets a risk score from behavioral and account signals, so suspicious entries are flagged for review before prizes are paid.
  • Admins can also run /refleader in the bot's DM to check standings without posting in the group.
  • Day campaigns run their full length: a 7-day campaign started Monday morning ends the next Monday at 19:00 local time.
  • The bot hypes the campaign for you: a daily standings post with a countdown (final day becomes a "last call"), and milestone shout-outs when an inviter hits every Nth valid referral. Both are controlled from /menu → Referral System — the milestone step is pickable so large groups aren't flooded.

Joining in (members)

  • /ref or /getref gives a personal invite link.
  • Invited users are greeted with a quick human check: they start muted, tap the verify button to open the bot's DM, and type the code from a captcha image to unlock chat. Verifying takes seconds and never counts toward validity — it just proves a human arrived. Anyone still unverified is auto-unmuted after an hour.
  • An invited user counts as valid once they post 3 meaningful messages within 24 hours of joining — stickers, emoji-only or one-word filler, forwards and repeated texts don't count.
  • /myref shows personal stats, /refleader the current top 10, /alltimeref the lifetime standings.
  • /refrules shows the campaign's payout terms; /refhelp lists the commands.

X Watch

Link your project's X (Twitter) account and the bot announces notable new followers in the group — an image card with the follower's avatar, your logo, their audience size, account age, and bio, plus clickable profile links. Multiple follows in one check arrive as a single message, with the overflow in a tap-to-expand block.

  • Setup in DM: /menu → 📡 X Watch → type the handle (the bot auto-detects it and confirms) → pick the alert threshold (1,000–20,000 followers) → switch ON.
  • Turning ON snapshots current followers first, so existing history never floods the chat — only people who follow from that moment count.
  • Each check reads the newest 20 followers. Accounts that can gain more than that between checks can request the 200-follower premium window straight from the menu.

Backup and upkeep

  • /backup exports the group's whole configuration (filters, welcome, buttons, logging, timed messages, restrictions) as a file. Reply to that file with /restore in any group to import it.
  • /cleandeleted sweeps members whose Telegram accounts were deleted. It can also run automatically once a day, toggled in /menu.
  • /report, available to every member as a reply to a spam image, feeds a crowdsourced review queue: repeated flags across groups reach the bot owner.
